## Requested scoped decision

For an actual formal callable `U_b` supplied at a Parameter/Lambda boundary, identify which dependent effect field of its complete invocation observation is governed by the composed-negative concrete Function effect selector `h` written in the annotation.

This asks only for the original annotation-to-invocation-field correspondence. It does not reopen the approved meaning of negative concrete annotations as permission for function-local subtraction, select a new source-admission rule, or authorize compiler implementation.

## Background and current premises

- The approved annotation policy says that a composed-negative concrete `[E]` permits function-local subtraction of that concrete effect from the effect variable at the governed position; positive `[E]` allows concrete `E`. The original governed occurrence, attachment, symbolic connections, and independent surviving contributions remain significant (`notes/design/2026-10-10-annotation-effect-hygiene-integration.md` §1 and §4).
- The native signature formation authority requires actual annotation interpretation, governed-port correspondence, permission, and boundary realization as independent inputs; it does not define the correspondence (`notes/design/2026-10-08-native-signature-formation-definition.md` §4).
- The reviewed field-map derivation constructs two candidate correspondences and checks the adopted Parameter/Lambda, producer-entry, Function, annotation, signature, and Generalize clauses. Those clauses retain complete invocation checking but do not choose which invocation subfield `h` governs. Its entry-effect/body-pure discriminator is conditional on separate carrier admission, challenge, resumption, and local-realization premises; it proves neither an admitted source witness nor an outward scheme difference (`notes/theory/2026-10-13-negative-formal-entry-effect-field-maps.md` §§1–5, 7).
- Complete invocation checking must retain entry effects. The unresolved question is whether this particular written permission also applies to an authentic entry contribution, or governs only the body-effect subfamily. Complete checking alone does not settle that permission map.

## Options and consequences

### A. Map `h` to the complete invocation effect field, including entry

The written selector governs the dependent effect field of the actual callable's complete invocation observation. Its entry contribution is within this permission's field when the original typed insertion and attachment establish that incidence. The full invocation obligation remains checked, and subtraction still requires the original local realization evidence.

Consequence: an authentic entry-only contribution can be eligible for this annotation's local negative permission, once its source incidence and realization are established. This does not prove such a contribution is admitted by the source rules or that it is actually removed.

### B. Map `h` only to the body-effect subfamily

The written selector governs the actual callable's body-effect subfamily under the original body-reach guard. Entry remains a separate field of the complete invocation observation and must still be checked, but an entry-only contribution receives no subtraction permission from this `h`.

Consequence: in the conditional entry-effect/body-pure discriminator, this map gives the entry-only contribution no subtraction permission from `h`, while A would make it eligible if the authentic incidence and realization premises held. The annotation's complete source contract must still state how its separately checked entry field is constrained; this option does not permit dropping entry from complete checking.

### C. Specify another exact dependent-field map

State the exact field or fields selected by `h`, their source-owned injections and reach guards, and how every other field of complete invocation checking remains constrained. Preserve the original occurrence, attachment, provider and same-environment realization evidence.

Consequence: an unspecified “whole function” or “body only” description is insufficient to construct or verify the original Parameter/Lambda annotation law.
