## Conditional results

### C: generated shared-body Pure-value callback lift

The additional hypothesis is an explicit source generator, not the desired
whole-bound containment. Its finite monomorphic decorated core preserves
immutable lexical roots, actual introduction/entry, local primitive contracts,
the existing owner/view kernel, and all source identities. The complete bound
is built by positive source relation constructors with joint witness hiding.
The checked lift copies those constructors and adds only total-function
definitions of fresh logical projection coordinates. It cannot add a new
restriction against an independently chosen fixed target endpoint.

The constructor lemma proves monotonicity and conservative extension of every
finite bound derivation, including local conservative alternatives. The
extension leaves both the old witness and complete source observation intact.
Least finite-observation derivations and all finite latent developments pin
the recursive interpretation. This closes the segment-slack gap for this
specified generator; mere AST/node preservation would not.

Independent punctured-source certificates generate the challenge domain.
Initial carrier, response, repeated resumption, and future-use admission use
local source ports, original operation witnesses, and the current owner/view;
none assumes the comparison being proved. A Unit/identity instance explicitly
shows that the definition is not vacuous. At one fixed `nu,K,D` the proof is

```text
D_checked subset D_actual
for each h in D_checked: P_actual(h) subset P_checked(h).
```

The selected `d-`, `d+`, and `b+` remain distinct occurrences. The printed
`[b,d]` is the linked output projection of that same complete relation, not
an independently reinterpreted row. The original Pure value remains Pure;
Value entry executes the whole argument's designated computation and rebinds
once. Callback literal B and its independent endpoint synthesis are unchanged.

The result covers the generated shared-body lift, not every unrelated
annotation with the same printed Function ports. State, opaque providers,
internal handler images, arbitrary adapters, unbounded polymorphic instances,
and the correspondence to full raw/production Yulang generation remain outside
the theorem. Ambient source-typed handler behavior is the same supplied kernel
on both sides.

### S: regular completion with grounded components and one open anchor

A separate source-generation induction defines a finite monomorphic pure
structural shadow, including records, projections, finite annotations, and
recursive references. After successful rational equality and known-head
normalization, connect free classes by their original free/free bounds.
Every component must have either only closed descriptor anchors (G), or exactly
one distinct open descriptor anchor (A). All input rigid names must be
permitted at every free class for the completeness theorem.

Keep G variables separate and solve their entire root-only package by the
existing finite synchronous construction. For one witness, alias each A
component to its unique anchor and attach the chosen G regular graphs. Open
anchors may recursively reach their own component, other A components, and G
components, through arbitrary admitted variance paths. Alias elimination
leaves a finite constructor-guarded graph. Each A bound becomes reflexive;
each G bound retains its independently checked directed obligation. Hence

```text
full arbitrary-tree satisfiability
iff G root-only satisfiability
iff G regular satisfiability
iff full regular satisfiability.
```

The finite construction terminates and needs at most `N+M` graph nodes, where
`N` is the original quotient size and `M` is the selected root-only witness
size. It proves existence, not preservation of the complete solution fiber.
Original inequalities and residual constraints are never replaced by the
witness's temporary equalities.

The self-application shadow has `X <: Function(X,R)`, yielding
`R={}`, `X=Function(X,{})`; it therefore retains genuine open recursive
Function feedback. Further examples cover mutual recursion, Record width,
contravariance, and A-to-G dependencies. A narrower-cap counterexample explains
why rejecting a forbidden-rigid equality candidate would be incomplete.

This is separate from the concurrently added empty-Record source fragment:
that result and its generator/carrier boundary remain intact. The new theorem
admits nonempty mandatory Records and proves arbitrary-to-regular reflection
under its different incidence and scope predicates.
