# Callback theorem under a source-checkable recipe premise

Date: 2026-10-04
Status: conditional proof record; no new semantic authority
Scope: Pure-value callback / Function adequacy main gate
Depends on: Authoritative callback-context-delivery §§2–2.1, typed-core §9,
source-interface-adequacy §4, and the direct main-gate attacks

## Result

The failed proof isolates the obstruction: an execution derivation cannot
invert a conservative complete bound. The useful additional assumption must
constrain how that bound is generated, without assuming the desired global
inclusion. The earlier CERR formulation below is not itself such a finite
check: its unrestricted “Local lifting” clause quantifies over every segment
witness and would require a semantic inclusion proof.

The smallest useful generator discipline found so far is the positive
relational presentation and syntactic lift of the reviewed source-generated
theorem package, §§2.4–2.6. Its finite source check verifies constructor
forms, shared operand tuples and binder scopes, source labels, and that every
new coordinate is defined by a total function of the old tuple. Primitive
leaves must be shared unchanged or carry their independent source
certificates. This is a sufficient premise exposed by the failed
`{0}`/`{0,1}` bound countermodel, not a claim of absolute weakestness.

For the existing source-recipe induction, separate the generator check from
its premises:

1. **Admission:** each checked challenge is admitted by independently emitted
   source rules at the same `nu,K,D`; admission does not depend on success of
   the inequality being proved.
2. **Generated lift:** copy each whole relation constructor, old operand
   tuple, shared hidden coordinate, and binder scope. Add only fresh
   coordinates defined by total functions on the copied tuple. Do not add a
   predicate on old coordinates, a query-success test, or an independent
   complete-call bound leaf. This syntactic rule gives conservative extension
   by induction, including witnesses for conservative local slack.
3. **Composition:** the complete endpoint is the existing state-threaded
   composition of those constructors; the `d+` and `b+` row components retain
   their original descriptors and `nu,K,D` links, with no independent
   complete-call bound leaf.

Admission is a separate source-certificate construction from a punctured
context and its local carrier/provider/history rules; it does not use the
tested inequality. The finite graph audit checks the recipe and lift syntax,
not arbitrary semantic witness inclusion. The common-argument-endpoint
restriction below is one simple source-checkable way to provide the actual
Value-entry inlet premises in the bounded fragment; it is not an equality
imposed by adaptation.

This is minimal relative to the counterexample and this compositional proof:
independent admission yields domain inclusion without query success; the
positive graph/lift rule excludes root-only widening while retaining local
slack; composition connects those witnesses to the complete checked endpoint.
It is not proved weakest among all possible generator contracts. The nine
CERR checks below elaborate the bounded-fragment obligations, but any clause
about denotation must be discharged by the syntactic graph/lift theorem or an
independent source certificate, not by a finite topology check alone.

For each callback invocation, the positive graph is assembled from the
existing entry/argument, typed rebind, body/result, and designated
result-consumer constructors by ordinary state-threaded bind equations. The
source check inspects generation clauses and graph references; it does not
decide arbitrary semantic adequacy. In the bounded first-order instance, the
following locate the exact obligations:

1. link the independently generated segment endpoints to those constructors;
2. for this bounded fragment only, the slot's checked argument descriptor
   and the actual Pure value's §21 Value-entry descriptor are the same
   source-owned endpoint; this is a syntactic endpoint-identity restriction,
   not an equality imposed by adapting a value;
3. carry one `ν` and the existing `K,D`, occurrence/incidence, receipt,
   `Flow`/`Observe`, and subtraction evidence through every edge;
4. close every bound-permitted return, request, and typed resumption under
   rebind and the next segment, including conservative slack successors; and
5. preserve the generated value roots and return members at each constructor;
   in this bounded fragment these are first-order data, with no latent
   callable/thunk future-use obligation; and
6. use the same generated relation leaf and complete old operand tuple on
   both sides for each argument, body, and result-consumer segment; and
7. the canonical flat output row `[b,d]` retains each component descriptor
   and its `ν,K,D` references from the `d⁺` and `b⁺` occurrences, with the
   existing occurrence/path links; it does not reconstruct, filter, or
   independently approximate those components; and
8. the bind constructor passes its generated result coordinate to the typed
   rebind/body continuation, including after each permitted resumption; and
9. introduce no independent complete-call bound leaf.

The finite recipe and shared endpoint identities are checked from the
endpoint-generation clauses and their constructor references. Local
relations may retain conservative slack because the lift copies the same
relation and entire tuple. A condition that merely says each segment bound
covers all its source observations remains semantic; it is not established
by graph shape. Under the generator rule, no separate local-inclusion theorem
is needed: inversion retains the same leaf witness and the typed bind rule
retains its same continuation input. `Flow`/`Observe` still locate event
occurrences and the original `[b,d]` coordinates; they do not create that
coordinate map.

The endpoint-identity restriction alone does not establish equality of
complete challenge domains: those domains also include owner/configuration
and response/resumption admission. The premise supplies only the needed
`D_checked ⊆ D_actual`, by mapping each independently certified checked
challenge to the actual Value-entry inlet. It makes no claim for distinct
parameter endpoints related by variance or adaptation.

**Conditional theorem.** Fix one fiber `ν,K,D`. Suppose the actual complete
bound has a positive source-labelled presentation
`P_actual(X) = exists Z. F_G(X,Z)`. Suppose checked generation copies every
constructor, old tuple, shared coordinate, and binder scope of `F_G`, and
adds only total definitions of fresh coordinates. Let the selected
`d⁺`/`b⁺` projection read the original argument/body contributions without
filtering them, and suppose the challenge certificates are generated
independently of the tested inequality and map to actual Value-entry inlets.
Formally, the checked relation is
`F_checked(X,Z,W) = F_G(X,Z) ∧ Def_T(W;X,Z)`, where `Def_T` consists only of
the generated total-coordinate equations and the complete observation
projection forgets `W` without changing any old field.
Then

```text
D_checked(ν,K,D) ⊆ D_actual(ν,K,D)
∀h ∈ D_checked. P_actual(h;ν,K,D) ⊆ P_checked(h;ν,K,D).
```

The inclusion covers all finite generated observation derivations, including
those enabled by conservative primitive slack, and retains their selected
linked `[b,d]` projection. It does not claim equality of complete challenge
domains.

This is a deliberately restricted sufficient fragment, not a minimum-
assumption theorem for general Function adaptation. In particular, the shared
argument endpoint excludes distinct parameter endpoints related only by
variance/adaptation. No necessity claim is made for this restriction.

**Proof.** A checked certificate is also an actual inlet certificate by the
independent admission premise, so domain inclusion does not use query success.
For the bound clause, take a joint witness for `F_G` and invert its finite
constructor derivation. At each node, retain the complete old operand tuple
and binder witnesses. Evaluate the added total-coordinate definitions on
that tuple; they impose no new condition on it. Bind and request constructors
retain the same result or pending suffix, so induction extends to every finite
resumption history. The complete observation and original occurrence IDs are
unchanged. The selected projection therefore remains admitted by the linked
`[b,d]` view. Existential hiding happens once around the joined graph, so no
shared witness is split. No row-union law, exact primitive bound, new carrier,
or concrete-comparison transitivity is used.

The theorem is deliberately bounded: it does not cover nested escaping
closures, latent future behavior, arbitrary higher-order challenge values,
or State/import worlds. Extending it requires applying the same source
constructor generation and challenge-admission proof recursively, not
assuming the whole Function theorem.

## Separate source-generation result

There are two generators here and they must not be conflated. Typed-core §9
does give a source-level **operational graph** construction: for every
resolved ordinary core callable, allocate its carrier port and link the
actual entry, bind, body, result consumer, and return delimiters; recursive
references reuse graph nodes. Induction over its finite derivation graph
proves that this operational translation satisfies the Composition graph
shape check: each expression constructor emits its specified node and links
its child labels, while recursive references reuse preallocated labels. This
is a separate source-generation theorem and does not inspect an inequality or
a solved assignment.

Theorem C §§2.3–2.6 separately proves finite generation and witness lifting
for its explicitly defined typed-core-to-relational generator: preallocate
source labels, emit only the listed positive constructors, and construct the
checked lift by copying tuples/scopes plus total-coordinate definitions. This
is the requested source-generation theorem for that mathematical generator.
It proves the stated premise by construction, rather than assuming a regular
solution or the callback inclusion.

It does not prove that the finite **inference endpoint** has that shape,
satisfies Admission and Local lifting, or bounds the operational graph. §9
explicitly leaves the symbolic complete image as an inference obligation.
The conditional theorem above concerns that finite endpoint, so the
operational graph theorem cannot discharge it.

Neither theorem identifies that mathematical relational generator with the
production finite inference endpoint. The current `yu-hir::ResolvedExpr`
has no application/call, request, handler, or explicit computation-consumer
node, and its simple-chain lowerer rejects non-leaf `Apply`; the proved raw
source map covers only monomorphic pure value bindings. More decisively, the
authoritative B contract does **not specify the finite endpoint generator**:
B requires
independent parameter/body/result synthesis and one final `F_lit <: F_cb`,
but callback design §2.1 step 6 leaves formation of the completed interface
as an obligation. Typed-core §9 supplies the operational `J_call` image for a
resolved ordinary semantic interface and expressly does not construct a
finite presentation for every caller. Source-interface-adequacy §4 proves
bind lifting for a supplied adequate interface; it does not identify the
syntax-generated endpoint with that interface.

This is an underdetermination theorem about the finite endpoint-generation
contract: a finite abstraction that maps the operational links to the same
entry/rebind/body/result constructors, locally covers each source segment,
and preserves their joint bound can satisfy the premise; a completion that
independently adds a conservative
complete-call bound leaf still satisfies B's stated endpoint independence
and final inequality ordering, but can admit an observation without segment
factorization. The current documents specify neither finite abstraction.
Therefore no theorem that the finite Yulang inference endpoint satisfies
CERR follows yet, and no Yulang source counterexample follows either.

The exact unresolved item is the bridge from raw Yulang/HIR generation to the
finite endpoint emitted after B step 5. A production source-generation
theorem must show, constructor by constructor, that it emits the positive
relational graph, independent challenge admission, and total-coordinate lift
above. Current B and HIR do not establish that premise. This is a missing
finite-generation contract, not a user semantic choice and not a reason to
add new evidence machinery. Until that bridge is proved, the unrestricted
Pure-value callback/Function adequacy gate remains open.
