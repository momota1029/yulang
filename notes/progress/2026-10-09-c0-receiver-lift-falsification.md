## Two discriminating rule-elimination routes

### Final hard-envelope violation

Suppose the actual complete membership grammar has the independently
certified §3.7 form at the original tuple telescope:

```text
H_G(R)(y) iff G(y) and
  (R(y) or Z(y) or exists x,z. H_G(R)(x) and W(x,y,z)).
```

**Conditional theorem:** every original finite membership derivation
`d : H_G(R)(y)` eliminates to `g : G(y)` at the same final tuple `y`.
The proof is last-rule elimination: source, Z and W constructors each retain
the final guard; W's predecessor guard is not substituted for it. Conjunction,
scope and legal whole-map wrappers retain that guard at their actual shared
tuple/substitution. Thus a witness violating a conjunct already included in
this complete G cannot inhabit this certified membership grammar.

The exact premise is an authentic exhaustive grammar with the **complete**
output-dependent G and its original guard evidence. Applying this argument to
an unspecified ArmDecl_e without that premise would assume the missing source
rule. The conditional theorem proves neither that every original Q_e has this
grammar nor that boundary success establishes its G. It uses no executable
oracle and makes no independence claim about supplied transition rules.

For the successful seed above, trying to append a Bool result outside Int,
or a guarantee-violating effect, does not produce a source witness. There is
no selected arm rule licensing that append; a certified §3.7 grammar with
those original output requirements would reject it at the final guard. The
receiver's legitimate DataArg alternatives are already handled by its VP
proof and cannot be relabeled as a new complete Call arm.

### Missing continuation, admission or future action

These obligations are not discharged merely by positivity of H_G. Source
contracts §3.7 condition 4 and Theorem IF §3.1 require each changing arm's
actual output-provider/current-world domain certificate and its original
future/response schema. Theorem IF preserves that evidence when supplied;
it does not create it. A provider with the same descriptor is not a substitute.

Consequently this route requires a concrete licensed original arm and an
independently admitted development at its actual interface before one can
show failure. Neither is specified by the pinned complete Call constructor.
Removing a future certificate from a complete interface, asserting membership
for an undeclared clause, or transplanting another provider's admission would
describe a defective hypothetical contract; it would not establish a legal
selected-source witness. Absence of a construction in these notes is also
insufficient to prove semantic nonexistence.

For pending cases the permitted equations remain:

```text
Request(q,C,k_f) >>= S_h
  = Request(q,C,(response,C'). k_f(response,C') >>= S_h)

Request(q,C,k_arg) >>= S_after_entry
  = Request(q,C,(response,C'). k_arg(response,C') >>= S_after_entry).
```

The first keeps the unreached receiver/carrier/result interface in `S_h`.
The second keeps the original rebind/body/native-return/designated-consumer/
invocation-return suffix in `S_after_entry`; receipt has already occurred and
is not replayed. Resume uses that handle's actual current C'. Omitting a source
Call consumer, changing a response port or resuming at the old world is not a
legal mutation of these selected structural rules. A nonstructural arm can
have its own original continuation action, but that action must first be
named from its declaration. No returning-only or source-diagonal reduction is
used to avoid pending cases.
