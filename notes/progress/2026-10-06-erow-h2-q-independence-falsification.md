## Objective and exact result

Attack the proposed H2 shortcut: successful target comparison `Q` supplies,
or uniquely identifies, the annotation-occurrence-to-role/polarized-port/profile
correspondence. The result is conditional on two independently supplied
decorations having equal comparison domains and observation bounds. Under
those assumptions, the same semantic comparison judgment and its successful
verdict are compatible with distinct original source correspondences. No
decoder of that judgment/verdict alone can recover both correspondences.

This is a **conditional non-identification result for a finite supplied
model**, not a raw-source counterexample, an ambiguity in an accepted source
contract, or a theorem that production comparison erases profiles. The finite
model establishes consistency of the explicitly listed comparison equations;
it does not establish that source typing/admission generates either decoration.

There are two meanings of “same Q” that must be distinguished. Here it means
the same extensional comparison inputs `(A,C,xi)` and success observation.
If a concrete query record includes its complete decorated endpoints, original
source addresses and the correspondence certificate, the records below differ.
Such a record can return a supplied correspondence; it has not derived that
correspondence from comparison success. No equal-full-record claim is made.

The selected authority already forbids manufacturing source evidence from Q.
It does not supply a complete correspondence rule that makes the two-world
conditional argument inconsistent. A future source rule may determine which
world a given source belongs to; that rule is exactly the additional input
the shortcut lacks.

## Minimal hypotheses

Fix one joint assignment `xi=(nu,K,D)` and a nonempty finite comparison fiber.
These hypotheses are not source elaboration rules:

1. **Supplied original positions.** A completed higher-order typed signature
   has a callback formal with two positions
   `p0=arg.call.effect` and `p1=arg.result.function.call.effect`, `p0 != p1`.
   Both have sign `-`: the offered receiver has sign `+`, receiving its
   callback reverses sign, and returning a callable preserves that sign.
   The local depths are respectively 0 and 1. Actual callable roles and entry
   modes are fixed identically in both worlds before comparison.
2. **Candidate source decorations.** In world `W0`, one concrete annotation
   occurrence `sigma0` has a source-derived correspondence to `p0`; in world
   `W1`, `sigma1` corresponds to `p1`. Each grants only the supplied concrete
   contract `W=write int` at its own position. Both positions are protected;
   the other position has no concrete grant. Static source identities, scope,
   profile entries, and evidence roots are supplied before Q. No row, family
   head or successful query creates these objects. Admitted annotation typing,
   completed contract formation and source existence of these decorations are
   **unproved premises**, not results of the model.
3. **Comparison equality.** The comparison projections of these two supplied
   decorations have the same complete admissible challenge domains and the
   same joint observation bounds at `xi`. Thus they supply the same
   extensional endpoints `A,C` to Q. This is a **candidate assumption**, not
   established source/production correspondence. It is stronger than equal
   printed types, equal row support or both queries returning success.
4. **No hidden identifying input.** The proposed decoder receives only
   `(A,C,xi,Q-success)`. It receives neither source occurrence/address/depth
   nor the original decorated profile/correspondence. Granting those inputs
   would change the proposed shortcut into reading independently supplied
   source evidence.

Hypothesis 2 does not assert both mappings for one fixed source occurrence.
The two source worlds are distinct. In each, all abstract components, if any,
must retain their original joint assignment and incidence; no port witness
is freshened independently. The model does not interpret a full mixed row,
derive a grant from `write int`, or replace the approved targeted-removal rule.

## Smallest supplied model and derivation

The distinction is explicit in the original profiles:

| World | Annotation source depth | Correspondence | Profile at p0 | Profile at p1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W0 | sigma0 at depth 0 | sigma0 -> p0, sign - | protected; explicit W contract | protected; no concrete grant |
| W1 | sigma1 at depth 1 | sigma1 -> p1, sign - | protected; no concrete grant | protected; explicit W contract |

For a concrete finite interpretation of hypothesis 3, stipulate independently
of the mapping the singleton challenge set `D0={d*}`. Its one complete history
invokes the outer callable on a pure Unit carrier, obtains a returned callable,
invokes it on a pure Unit carrier and returns Unit. Its complete observation
`tau` retains those receipts/entries/returns and has no request. These are
supplied typed-core interaction labels, not asserted accepted raw syntax.
In each world supply exactly these semantic comparison bounds:

```text
D_A = D_C = {d*}
P_A(d*) = P_C(d*) = {tau}
Q(A,C;xi): D_C subseteq D_A and forall d in D_C, P_A(d) subseteq P_C(d)
```

Both clauses of Q hold by equality, with the same nonempty fiber and the same
complete observation, rather than merely the same empty effect support.
Neither concrete grant is exercised because there is no request in this
supplied history. No operation declaration, handler selection or response
witness is required to evaluate these set equations.

The domain is a supplied restricted finite model, **not** all callers, all
future histories or production Function membership. In particular, admitting
effectful arguments or future uses may distinguish the two decorations and
invalidate hypothesis 3. The model does not derive a rejection of such clients
from source types. Equality of these finite projections establishes only the
conditional non-identification premise; it is not an ordinary-input support
envelope decision.

Let `decode(A,C,xi,success)` select a single original port/profile. Its input is
identical in W0 and W1, so its output is identical. If it chooses p0 it fails in
W1; if it chooses p1 it fails in W0. Choosing neither fails to identify either
correspondence. Returning both is an uncertainty set, not the required exact
source correspondence, and attaching W to both invents an extra grant in each
world. Therefore an exact Q-only decoder does not exist under hypotheses 1–4.
For a randomized decoder, no single identical input distribution guarantees
the two different exact answers with probability one.

This is minimal for the **distinct depth/port** obstruction: two worlds, one
annotation occurrence in each, two original effect positions, one concrete
contract, one nonempty challenge, and zero requests. One position cannot
exhibit two different port/depth outputs; one world cannot refute a selector
that always selects its answer. The outer and returned call observations both
occur in tau, so the distinction is not hidden by omitting the returned call.
No general minimality over other role/scope/occurrence obstructions is claimed.

## Independence, mutations and stopping conditions

There is no executable oracle. The set equations are checked by the displayed
derivation. Both worlds share the supplied challenge/observation equality,
the original assignment, fixed roles/entry, and the candidate source-admission
premises. These are not independently established by an implementation. A
checker that implements these equations could confirm the calculation but
could not prove that source elaboration creates either world or that arbitrary
source comparison factors through this projection.

Named textual mutations, not executed tests:

- Select every matching concrete row at p0: W1 is the failing branch.
- Flatten occurrence depth or reconstruct grants from family W: both profiles
  become indistinguishable, losing the required original positions.
- Copy the grant to both ports after success: introduces the unauthorized
  grant at the other port in each supplied world.
- Include the source certificate in the decoder input: discriminates worlds
  correctly, while explicitly retaining the missing source producer premise.
- Add a request at one position and an independently typed matching owner:
  domains/observations may differ. The equal-Q premise must be re-proved, so
  this note makes no claim for that extension.

Failure conditions: the conditional source decorations cannot be admitted;
the same joint assignment cannot support them; roles/entry or complete domains
actually differ; a semantic comparison includes profile-sensitive observations
omitted by the singleton interpretation; or Q is defined to include the full
source certificate. The last condition makes recovery a certificate lookup,
not source derivation by successful comparison.

This attempt stops at the explicit source/admission and equal-denotation
premises. It does not propose another supplied-transition probe: larger finite
case counts would leave those premises untouched. Unverified scope includes
raw grammar/HIR acceptance, actual annotation formation, arbitrary mixed-row
membership, source/production adequacy, open or recursive clients, attachment,
deep images, principality and implementation. No independent review or gate
closure is claimed.

Recommended next action: require the H2 producer to accept the original
source occurrence/depth and completed contract, construct their correspondence
before Q, and explain preservation through inference/generalization/use.
Retain this two-position instance as a discriminator for any proposed Q-only
shortcut; do not seek larger transition-model evidence for source formation.
