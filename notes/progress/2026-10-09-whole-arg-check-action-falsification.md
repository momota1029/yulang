## Conditional theorem: faithful common evidence excludes asymmetry

Fix two complete consumer indices j_N,j_O, a common legal action theta, and
the corresponding entire kernel indices q_N,q_O. Assume:

1. Authentic scoped reference/realization maps identify q_N and q_O with one
   complete kernel index q and identify the action induced there. If the maps
   are reindexings, their reference substitutions commute with theta; identity
   of a kernel name alone is insufficient.
2. There are checking projections `p_N: W_N(j_N)->K(q)` and
   `p_O: W_O(j_O)->K(q)` obeying the displayed component naturality laws.
   These are retained evidence projections, not functions that reconstruct
   witnesses by solving an endpoint proposition.
3. A candidate witness pairing H preserves that same evidence:
   `p_O(H(z))=p_N(z)`. The original projection p_O is injective on the paired
   checking subfamily, including its transported image. Equivalently, an
   authentic contextual wrapper there has an inverse returning the whole
   retained evidence. For full tuple parity, every other dependent field
   requires its corresponding faithful retained map as well.

Then, wherever both transported sides are defined,

```text
p_O(H(theta_N z)) = p_N(theta_N z)
                   = rho(theta)(p_N(z))
                   = rho(theta)(p_O(H(z)))
                   = p_O(theta_O H(z)).
```

Injectivity gives `H(theta_N z)=theta_O H(z)` on this checking subfamily.
No independent action law for H has been assumed: it follows from faithful
retention of identical evidence and the one kernel's two projection laws.
Existence of H, equality of its images and its exact incidence premises have
not been proved. This is not a construction of full decorated attachment.

Apply this theorem to the prior note's assumed two-element complete fibers,
where each member differs only in its exposed `e_a`, and assume the faithful
same-evidence pairing above. If s fixes j_N,j_O and the original paired
members, then

```text
p_N(s_N n) = rho(s)(p_N n)
           = rho(s)(p_O(H n))
           = p_O(s_O H n) = p_N n.
```

Since the N members differ only in that evidence, s_N fixes them too. A
transposition is impossible under these exact premises. The derivation works
for any cardinality and any stabilizer; it adds no finite witness model.

## Why sharing the kernel alone is weaker

There are three distinct situations:

| Situation | Licensed conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| Both uses retain the entire identical kernel fiber at the same complete index and same legal action, with faithful contextual wrappers | Wrapper identification is equivariant; swap/fix asymmetry is excluded. |
| Same kernel but different full dependent indices or differently induced actions | Within-kernel identity/composition says nothing sufficient about a cross-incidence pairing; inspect actual scoped reference maps. |
| Same kernel index/action but complete consumer constraints select different witness subfamilies | The common action restricts to each subfamily; their restricted representations need not agree without matching witness images. |

The third row matters because N §5 allows `e_out` to depend on `e_f,e_a`.
Writing a complete tuple schematically as `(e_a,e_out,...)`, the available
checking image is

```text
I_N = { a in K(q) | a extends to an admitted complete N tuple },
I_O = { a in K(q) | a extends to an admitted complete original tuple }.
```

The statement `|W_N|=|W_O|=2` supplies neither `I_N=I_O` nor a matching
extension rule. Even if theta stabilizes both consumer indices, equality of
cardinality does not determine the restrictions of rho to these images.
An identical kernel cannot be assigned two independent actions at one
witness; it can have different behavior on different retained witnesses.
No such differently admitted subfamilies have been constructed or licensed
for the actual literal source occurrence in this audit.

Therefore the precise blocker has two owner locations: the checking
declaration owns rho and the exact instantiated witness index; the source

map from N's occurrence to the original reference. An obstruction must come
