## Objective and result

For the approved `apply(f: _ -> [io] _, x) = f x`, isolate the minimum
information the owning source judgments must produce to connect the written
permission to a particular contribution and to realize a lawful change.
The approved sources support a conditional composition of those outputs.
They do not supply the missing incidence producer or the activation rule.
Permission, annotation checking, inference protection and actual event
handling have separate premises.

The reduced C6 cut is: **an authentic annotation-to-contribution certificate,
then an independently justified local change certificate with a frame law**.
A certificate retained at construction can remove later reconstruction work;
retaining it does not decide when a change is lawful. Neither global row
subtraction nor an annotated-implies-unprotected rule follows.

For a claim about an **actual handled request**, rather than annotation-local
evidence, append its independently typed request/contribution incidence,
actual provider, receipt and current eligible receiver/visibility derivation.
Raw resumption uses the then-live configuration and original handle. These
are event-specific inputs; a static slot or permission cannot create them.
Their existence is not imposed here as the universal activation condition for
an annotation-local change, because that condition is still unselected.

An authentic protection origin is required if the proposed change specifically
removes protection. The annotated-formal seed result prevents obtaining that
origin from the excluded absence lineage. It does not prevent an independently
justified origin, and it does not make permission contingent on first proving
that lineage. Thus permission can be recorded while a protection/change
obligation remains unresolved.

## Conditional construction with explicit hypotheses

H1. A finite set of actual annotation derivations supplies the `Ann` outputs
above, and source/typed derivations supply authentic `Contribution` outputs.
Original identities, scope and the same `xi` are retained.

H2. The owning annotation/use constructors supply exactly their authentic
`Incident` derivations, independently of `Q`, successful membership, observed
execution or solved endpoint equality. The incidence is interpreted actively
at the same original objects. This is the unresolved producer hypothesis;
the accepted permission does not prove H2.

H3. A realized boundary supplies genuine `Direct`, `Lawful` and `Frame`
proofs at those same objects. `Lawful` includes the missing activation rule,
and its independent justification is assumed, not proved here.

H4. Any generalization/use step is a certified whole map preserving the
original annotation, contribution, incidence, scope, dependencies and lawful
evidence together. Shared witnesses stay shared and required rigid imports
stay fixed. H4 is needed only for the transport corollary.

Construct a permission entry by joining `Ann`, `Contribution` and `Incident`
on their original identities, scope routes and joint assignment:

```text
Ann(a,b,beta,sigma,E,T,i)
Contribution(k,o,p,sigma,xi)
Incident(a,i;k,o,p;beta,sigma,xi)
-------------------------------------------------- conditional C6 permission
MayRemove(a,i,k;beta,sigma,p,xi)
```

Here `MayRemove` records the approved permission at exactly the certified
incidence. It neither changes a profile nor asserts that a request executes.
Different certified incident contributions give different entries. There is
no rule completing the fiber by matching every effect spelled `io`.

**Conditional incidence lemma.** Under H1–H2, the construction emits exactly
the permission entries justified by the supplied authentic annotation
incidences, preserving their scope, owner/orientation and dependencies.
Proof: every emitted entry has its three source premises by construction.
Conversely an authentic incidence supplies their matching original references,
so its entry is emitted. Distinct derivation witnesses may justify one entry;
no proof-choice quotient or uniqueness of incidence is inferred. No step
reads `Q`, invents receipt, or reverses an upper/lower edge. This is exactness
relative to H2's owning constructors, not raw-source completeness. QED.

At a realized boundary use the further proofs without reconstructing them:

```text
MayRemove(a,i,k;beta,sigma,p,xi)
Direct(a,E,T;epsilon)   Lawful(a,i,k,E,T;rho)   Frame(rho)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
export T with local (epsilon,rho), retain prior evidence and origins
```

**Conditional local realization lemma.** Under H1–H3, that export satisfies
the direct-boundary/target/evidence obligation and retains every unrelated
contribution and evidence item named by `Frame`. Proof: `Direct` is already
at the actual current `E` and complete `T`, so no transitive concrete-success
inference is used. The lawful proof specifies the same certified local action;
`Frame` supplies its preservation on all other objects. Appending its local
evidence preserves prior evidence and original incidence rather than replacing
them. The lemma says nothing about a numeric pop count, event execution or
arbitrary source acceptance. QED.

This lemma explicitly assumes the lawful transition. It is a composition and
retention theorem; it does **not** prove the source transition rule or make
`PermitLocal` sufficient for removal. Under H4, transport commutes with both
joins: apply the whole map to each premise and conclusion, retaining origin
references. The same proof works at the mapped scope. It does not restore an
exited maker's dynamic authority or introduce a new removal at each fresh use.

## Smallest remaining cut and failure conditions

The smallest dependency cut consists of one actual `[io]` annotation, one
authentic incident contribution and one genuine direct boundary proof.
These suffice for the conditional permission judgment. Removing `Lawful`
leaves realization open even in this singleton case; adding more Calls, row
elements or supplied transition traces would not close it. This is a missing
constructor site, not a compiler rejection or an executable counterexample.

The minimum-information distinction is discriminating: omit original
annotation/contribution correspondence and the premises cannot establish
that the intended `f` contribution is the one governed; omit the direct
current endpoint and an earlier endpoint can replace an actual boundary;
omit `Lawful` and permission is promoted to action; omit `Frame` and unrelated
effects or prior/provider evidence have no preservation proof. These are
failures of the proposed derivation, not assertions that no alternative
representation can retain equivalent information.

The exact open rule is an implication from the authenticated local source
package to an admissible `Lawful` derivation, with its activation conditions
and full frame law. The cited sources explicitly leave that implication
unspecified. They are not a closed source calculus from which exhaustive
nonexistence or an independent semantic countermodel can be claimed. This
lane therefore selects neither annotation-slot activation nor an exclusively
live-handler activation. It also supplies no third equivalent toy model of
those alternatives. Distinguishing their observable lawful behavior requires
the owning source rule or a genuine retained implementation/oracle bridge.

Failures include a changed annotation/dependency; wrong binder or scope;
incidence reconstructed by family/shape/Q success; independently recombined
`xi`; lost proof choices; incomplete normalized target; skipping an actual
boundary; provider-owned protection erased; receipt synthesized from a static
slot; or event evidence reused after receiver exit. Failure of an input
certificate blocks this conditional claim, not the source program by a newly
invented rejection policy.
