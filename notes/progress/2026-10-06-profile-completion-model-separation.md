## A positive local normal form

Let `App_beta(xi)` denote **independently source-applicable original beta
positions**, retaining the source origins that establish applicability. This
notation exposes the input to the following result; it is not a definition
of applicability by whatever slots a candidate happened to return.

At every such position governed by the selected unannotated formal contract,
the original local policy has the following normal form:

```text
Original_beta(p;xi) =
  (original beta/source-origin/scope, p, FullProtection,
   AnnotationRemovalGrant=None, original K/D incidences)
  for p in App_beta(xi).
```

For the constructed source origin, `p0 in App_beta`. This policy neither makes
the effect row empty nor prescribes effect removal. Refining the internal
formal role does not create an annotation. A normalization that introduces a
beta-local concrete grant therefore directly contradicts call-view §§1.1/4.
Actual provider entry, and any independent provider profiles, remain intact.
The displayed normal form fixes the clause kind once its applicability is
established; it does not itself prove that seed erasure preserves every
solution or supplies the final receiving-view activation. Those are still
P/realization obligations. In particular, the initial seed is not silently
identified with a completed operational profile.

The typed-boundary realization then requires the same tagged packet image:

```text
chi_target = union_i M_i*chi_i
D_target   = union_i M_i*D_i
K_target   = the same shared predicate ledger
L_target   = inherited origin/runtime lineage
```

The sources `i` remain separate. In particular, a returned value can retain
its actual-value packet and an independently established callee-result
packet. Their union is **not** a statement that those packets are original
beta positions. Private captured fields are not exposed by transporting the
closure's public view. These are exactly typed-boundary §6's indexed-image,
result and private-field clauses.

For a dynamic instance `b` of this unannotated beta, actual `Receive`, matching
typed `Flow` and predispatch `Observe` establish `Path`. Current exact owner,
receiver and handler activity then establish `Inc_C`. Since this original
contract has no explicit operation admission:

```text
Grant_from_beta(q,h,C) = false.
```

This holds independently of the result/argument's latent type and of whether
an intervening handler would hide the request from outward support. It does
not say that the total `Grant(q,h,C)` is false: another independently valid
local concrete contract can grant that event under the selected no-origin-
veto rule. It also does not say that `Protected` is always true: that requires
the actual typed path, receipt and current activity at the particular event.

Predispatch observation is the existing clause
`Observe(q,o.view,o.port,o) iff o in views(EC(C_emit(q)))`. Thus changing an
outward row, or allowing an inner handler to consume an event, cannot erase
the initial complete-call observation or select a different original profile.
Observation creates no grant and no profile introduction.

This normal form proves that **any genuine P separation must change a
source-certified applicable-position/origin or its source-semantic constraint**.
It cannot arise just by choosing a different no-annotation grant,
seed-to-refined role label, receipt convention, packet image or outward-support
filter while retaining all selected clauses.

## Focused attempt 1: latent positions from unknown type shape

Suppose the result of `f x` is later solved to a Function or Thunk, or `A_x`
contains such a value. An attempted alternative adds a beta position there
because the solved descriptor now has a latent computation port.

The exact component supplies no elimination of `x` and no elimination of
the returned value of `f x`. Its only resolved Function elimination is `c`.
Charter §21 explicitly says Value entry does not recursively force latent
returned values. Typed-boundary §4 says latent construction enters no delimiter
for its future computation, and entering one view does not activate all its
nested latent positions. Inferred-call-view §2 disallows creating the requisite
source relations from type shape alone. Consequently this proposed origin
does not certify an extra original beta slot or its current observation.

A future call/force can have a real source origin. Source-contracts §3.3.4
requires that origin to name the actually returned provider and its original
typed port. That establishes the future-use path; it does not retroactively
establish that beta had a profile introduction at that port. Inferring the
latter from the former would omit exactly the source-applicability premise
under test. No future-context source derivation was substituted for it here.

**Defeated alternative:** shape-driven creation of original applicability or
current latent observations. **Not proved:** that every source-derived
omitted-annotation applicability rule must ignore a latent signature position.
The selected text does not provide that converse/exhaustiveness clause.

## Focused attempt 2: independently inherited origins masquerading as beta

The exact returned `step` gives a source-grounded capture route. The same
`f` packet travels into its lexical environment; later `step` invocation has
an actual captured-binding receipt. When its body executes `f x`, the original
beta `call.effect` position is observed in that executing complete view.
This accounts for later use without a new original beta position on the
public returned-`step` signature.

Alternatively, the actual result of `f x` may already have its own protected
latent profile. The indexed actual-value/result image retains it. Its
source origin can be real even though beta introduced nothing there.

The attempt to count either route as a new beta-origin position fails the
following exact rules from typed-boundary §6:

- Transport retains tagged source witnesses and does not allocate a boundary.
- `Receive` records use ownership and creates no boundary or concrete contract.
- Result transport maps `result.latent.effect` to returned `latent.effect`;
  it does not map `call.effect` there.
- A callee's public view is not pasted onto private bindings; transporting
  public closure views does not expose private capture fields.

Thus genuine inherited protection on a latent result is compatible with an
original beta inventory containing only the certified `p0`. It is not a
second P constructor with a larger original beta inventory. Erasing that
inherited evidence to force the **whole target packet** to be a singleton
would violate the same rules and is not the positive singleton completion.

**Defeated alternative:** enlarge or shrink original beta inventory by
reclassifying inherited packets, copying the immediate-call profile to latent
results, or treating capture/receipt as introduction. **Positive normal form:**
the beta-origin part of the certified constructor fragment is the `p0` image,
with all independently tagged inherited sources retained separately.

## Why this does not certify full P or require a new decision

The strongest positive inventory statement here is exact for the **certified
origin fragment**: its only established beta introduction is `p0`; Name,
capture, Bind and Result subsequently transport evidence rather than minting
another beta origin. This distinguishes original inventory from target-view
inventory even with unknown latent shapes.

Promoting this to `Slots_original(beta)={p0}` for every source solution requires
an origin-exhaustiveness rule for P. The selected documents expressly leave
the original applicable-position constructor to the next generation/proof
gate. Source-contracts' emission table does not close that premise: its
envelope already supplies the kernel's typed profiles and primitive contracts.
The initial construction likewise explicitly limits its singleton to
`InitialSeedSlots`.

Accordingly there is no demonstrated independent semantic choice between
two conformant local constructors. There is also no source rule in the
inspected clauses that proves that *all* possible P origins reduce to the
one certified elimination. A question about singleton versus all latent
descendants would again lack two certified alternatives and should not be
presented as necessary approval.

The next discriminating evidence is an exhaustive local P inversion theorem:
every beta-profile introduction in the exact component must be produced by a
named source rule, and that rule either reduces to the certified elimination
or identifies another independently applicable source contract position.
It must preserve original scopes, contribution associations and inherited
sources. This task has eliminated two tempting bogus origins; it has not
supplied that theorem or proved it impossible.
