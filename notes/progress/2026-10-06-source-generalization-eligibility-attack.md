## 1. Result and exact scope

For an ordinary projection lambda, source parameter registration, resolved
Name and Result synthesis determine a **family before generalization**:

```text
id x = x               input a; body/result a; no captured outer value root
pick y = z             input a; body/result b; captured Gamma(z)=Value(b)
```

The input endpoint `a` is freshly inferred by the ordinary parameter rule.
The output of `id` is the same endpoint, whereas `pick`'s output is the
captured endpoint `b`. This is a source conclusion, not a choice of an
already supplied eligible set. In the independently source-checked
projection envelope below, the endpoint-family reconstruction selects one
local template binder `a`, fixes the entire outer capture closure, and
places the binding around the **whole lambda view**. It does not give the
input and output separate copies or quantify a captured `b`.

This discharges the selected envelope's source-to-coordinate selection and
source-kernel reconstruction premises. Full semantic eligibility remains
conditional on complete typed constructor/admission laws and, for the
ordinary constrained-use route, whole-Function direct-resolution evidence.
Neither unrestricted all-view principality nor a raw-source generalization
policy closes. The result is stronger than alpha transport of a supplied
`Omega`: its family, repeated identity, ambient anchors and candidate
placement are derived from parameter/Name/Bind syntax. It is weaker than a
complete inferred Function scheme theorem.

The source/conditional decorated-core witnesses below also falsify three
particular shortcuts without stipulating an arbitrary Boolean relation:
maximal interface-free-variable
abstraction at an inner boundary, independent copies of a repeated port,
and replacement of a Bind/Name client join by unrelated per-call witnesses.
They distinguish structural/root and actual-value facts from bound precision;
no raw compiler acceptance claim is made. The `make` and `relay` witnesses
are conditional on the explicit decorated-core graphs in §§5.1 and 5.3;
their surface-brace realization remains open. This qualification does not
change PG-1's bounded projection endpoint-family result.

## 3. Independently meaningful projection envelope

Use ordinary, nonrecursive, unannotated value-parameter lambdas whose body is
a resolved Name, optionally preceded by a finite chain of ordinary local
bindings of returning Names. A terminal Name resolves either to the lambda's
formal or to one original outer value binding. No new source restriction is
adopted: this is a bounded proof envelope, not an acceptance/value-restriction
policy. General source Call bodies, recursive validation, handlers, adapters
and mutable captures remain outside this particular positive construction.

Outer values may themselves have latent providers. The proof does not erase
them or treat them as scalar data. Ordinary argument carriers can suspend or
diverge, and may execute effects at Value entry. The **body** is a returning
projection; the complete invocation is not assumed pure or terminating.

For each source lambda `L`, construct the following data from source rules:

1. Register `x:Value(a_L)` at its ordinary parameter binding, with the fresh
   inferred endpoint prescribed by the parameter rule.
2. Resolve every body Name to its original binding. A local alias receives
   the RHS result value through the original ordered Bind; its body lookup
   keeps that RHS endpoint and value witness.
3. Propagate the terminal Name's registered interface through Result.
   Consequently the body result endpoint is either exactly `a_L`, or exactly
   the original captured endpoint `b`. No second result coordinate is chosen.
4. Construct the original closure/body/entry view, retaining all source
   provider roots, receivers, receipts and typed metadata as dependent parts
   of its original constructor presentation. Do not quantify runtime provider
   identity or turn the input endpoint into a new provider.
5. Derive outer anchors `rho_L` by lexical capture and follow their original
   provider/environment/residual/admission/typed-path references transitively.
   This includes rigid coordinates not printed in `Fun`'s ports. Their
   original binder scopes remain fixed.

Step 4 still uses the reviewed constructors' independently typed context and
primitive admission meanings; syntax does not magically construct those
complete semantic leaves. But steps 1–3 determine which **value endpoint**
must repeat, and step 5 derives the relevant source capture anchors rather
than supplying a chosen `Omega` as input.

Call the resulting whole source kernel `G_L(rho_L,a_L)`. This notation
includes its full body, entry, argument Force, pending/return suffix,
provider/result and evidence relation. It is not the printed arrow alone.
Fresh inferred `a_L` is distinct from all captured identities at registration;
source kernel constraints can subsequently relate its value to an outer
coordinate. Such relations are retained, not a reason to split or erase a
coordinate. Dependence of a Function *term* on both `a_L` and a captured `b`
does not entail that their assigned values are equal.

### 3.1 The independent view check

Define a finite projection source view by checking this same resolved source
using ordinary parameter/Name/Result/Bind constructor rules, with a legal
parameter endpoint term `T` and the fixed original outer environment. The
checker follows each Name's binding and the ordered aliases; it never asks
whether the view is an instance of a proposed scheme or whether Q succeeds.
It can retain arbitrary additional well-scoped client constraints over its
whole tuple. It must retain the original actual role/entry, source provider,
complete non-value constructor/admission kernel and all scopes.

This is a kernel-aligned source view class, analogous to the explicit
`V_alloc` alignment boundary, not the class of all semantically sound
Function contracts. Its definition contains no generalized binder set or
instance map. It permits `T` to differ between independently checked views,
including distinct finite descriptor graphs, and does not require that one
monomorphic assignment handle every such `T` simultaneously.

## 4. PG-1: source endpoint selection and original-scope reconstruction

**Statement.** Relative to the typed constructors just identified, select
`a_L` as one local value-endpoint template parameter at `L`'s whole-view
boundary, with `rho_L` fixed. Every independently checked projection view
has an original-scope reconstruction of `G_L`, obtained by setting
`a_L := T`. The entire source-kernel witness is reconstructed from its own
source derivation, including any alias witnesses and full entry history.
For `id`, all formal/body/result occurrences use this same substitution.
For `pick`, only the input endpoint is substituted; the captured result and
all its dependencies remain the same original outer tuple.

**Proof.** Parameter registration supplies `T`. A terminal formal Name has
that registered `Value(T)` interface, so Result synthesizes `Comp(empty,T)`.
A terminal captured Name reads its original `Value(b)` binding, so Result
synthesizes `Comp(empty,b)` at the original scope. For a local alias, invert
the independent Bind derivation: the original RHS's actual result is rebound
to the alias before its suffix executes. Lookup and Return retain that same
result; induction on the finite alias chain yields the claimed endpoint and
the same actual descriptor/provider witness. No separately selected alias
result is needed.

The closure has the same source body and actual Value entry. Before argument
Return/rebind, its original argument carrier and pending suffix are retained;
there is no formal value available for a body projection yet. On actual
Return/rebind, the formal lookup returns that result, or the captured lookup
returns its fixed outer value. Entry effects, requests, responses and raw
resumptions are the original argument-history witnesses with the same
ordered suffix. The body adds no elimination of a latent returned value.
Thus every finite membership/admission witness in the independent aligned
constructor derivation fills the source kernel at the same tuple and scopes.
The source equations also give the reverse reconstruction of these aligned
constructor witnesses. All additional client conjuncts are kept unchanged.

The selection is consequently **not** “bind every free port”: the source
rules picked one fresh parameter, forced its repeated uses and fixed the
capture environment. Nor is it a theorem obtained by renaming a supplied
eligible set. Its key premise is independently meaningful: projection body
constructor inversion and complete kernel alignment, with no source body
operation that can inspect `T` to choose a different implementation.

### 4.1 What the proof does and does not finish

PG-1 derives endpoint-family soundness/reconstruction in this envelope. It
does not construct arbitrary primitive descriptor admission, all-world
context validity or guard classifications. For a complete generalized-view
result one must additionally show that the reconstructed whole lambda family
is admissible at the actual source Generalize boundary and that the existing
whole Function query has evidence at the **designated export** for every
independently checked view. In certified-use §5's notation, the remaining
clause is exactly:

```text
for every valid complete V and public v satisfying C_V:
  exists original-scope z.
    G_L^fresh(v,z) and Direct(R_L^fresh(v,z),R_V(v)).
```

PG-1 constructs the source-kernel part of this extension for its projection
class; it does not simply postulate `Ext_P(v)`. For identical reconstructed
complete constructor roots, a local whole-root reflexivity/congruence
certificate would discharge the direct query. No general resolver
completeness theorem or transitive concrete-success composition is inferred.
Views with widened guarantees, changed value conversions, different kernels
or additional production alternatives require their own comparison proof.

If later constraints force `a_L=b`, quantifying `a_L` while retaining that
equation yields no independently selectable value for it. The family remains
sound; deleting the equation would not. PG-1 therefore supplies a safe local
template candidate and reconstruction, not a claim of an irredundant maximal
semantic binder set for arbitrary residual constraints. Full principality
must establish coverage of every independently valid view and rule out
avoidable sharing across genuinely independent instantiation events.

Template binding is not `Intro22(a_L,l)`. Every actual/derived comparison
keeps its source classification and re-enters the level guard. No new
exception, level assignment or SCC-wide binder block is introduced here.

## 5. Source and conditional decorated-core falsification witnesses

The `id` witness uses the ordinary parameter/Name source rules. `make` and
`relay` below are **conditional decorated-core witnesses**, using typed core
§§2–3,6's derivation constructors with their typed premises. The displayed
brace spellings are candidate mnemonics only: the nested-block addendum's
approved `apply` interpretation does not extend to either spelling. Their
raw-brace-to-core correspondence, production acceptance and any broader
local generalization policy remain open.

### 5.1 Local allocation ancestry does not make an inner capture fresh

```text
my make z = { my pick y = z; pick }
```

Conditional intended constructor graph (abbreviate this data derivation as
`Make`):

```text
lambda(z,
  bind(pick,
    result(lambda(y, result(name z))),
    result(name pick)))
```

Here both parameters use ordinary Value entry; `pick`'s `name z` resolves to
the outer formal, and Bind stores and returns the local closure value. These
are hypotheses for this decorated graph, not a selected interpretation of
the displayed braces. Surface-brace realization remains open.

At `Make`'s boundary its ordinary formal endpoint `b` was locally allocated.
At `pick`'s boundary the same endpoint belongs to the fixed outer environment,
and its closure stores the original `z`. Name/Result synthesis therefore
gives `pick` input `a`, result `b`, not an independently fresh result `b_u`.
An incoming use of `Make` may instantiate its own template coherently;
`pick` must then retain that particular inherited instance. Historical
template ancestry and current ambient ownership are different.

Take `call(result(Make), result(literal 0))` under this decorated-core
constructor relation, conditional on its typed premises. Once its
argument returns, the returned `pick` captures that Integer descriptor. Any
later completed call of this `pick` returns the same captured Integer. A
scheme shortcut that freshly binds `b` inside `pick` and drops its capture
link can claim a Function-valued result for that actual closure, contrary
to the graph's Name/Return witness. This is a conditional semantic falsifier
for **that dropped-capture relation**, not proof that an actual compiler accepts the
bad use or that all internal type bounds must denote singleton values.

Even absence of `b` from a printed input port is insufficient: its provider,
latent result, environment and future-use admission references can retain
the dependency. Generalizing/hiding the captured value must either fix it or
retain one jointly scoped witness and the required admission certificate.
Nominal copying cannot manufacture a new capture.

### 5.2 One source formal is not two independently abstracted ports

```text
my id x = x
```

Parameter registration and terminal Name synthesis force one `a` in input
and body result. More strongly, actual Value entry followed by Name/Return
returns the same argument result descriptor and its entire provider/evidence.
Replacing the complete relation by independent input/result marginals can
admit an Integer argument with an unrelated Function result. The source
trace has no constructor capable of producing that Function.

This does **not** assert that every valid view of identity must print equal
input/output bounds: an output guarantee may legitimately be wider. The
failure is introducing a new actual result witness, or discarding the
original source link while claiming source-kernel preservation. Widened
bounds with the retained identity kernel need their direct whole comparison.

### 5.3 Client correlations arise from ordinary Bind and Name

```text
my relay h x = { my v = h x; h v }
```

Conditional intended constructor graph (abbreviate this data derivation as
`Relay`):

```text
lambda(h,
  result(lambda(x,
    bind(v,
      call(result(name h), result(name x)),
      call(result(name h), result(name v))))))
```

Both parameters use ordinary Value entry. The outer body returns the inner
closure; both `name h` occurrences in that closure resolve to the same outer
formal, and `name v` resolves to the first Call's result binding. The two
Call derivations require their typed callable/argument premises. This graph
is supplied as the conditional decorated-core witness; its displayed
surface-brace realization remains open.

The first Call returns a result value that the ordinary Bind stores in `v`.
The second Name supplies **that same actual result** as the second Call's
argument computation. Both `h` Names resolve to the same outer formal;
no independent generalized `h` introduction occurs inside this body. This
decorated graph derives a join between the two Call witnesses: original callee,
current state, first result/rebind and ordered suffix. It is an actual
constructor-derived client relation conditional on the graph and its typed
premises, not a Boolean `W` assumed by fiat.

For example, when the first result is an Integer, a stitched second witness
using an unrelated Function argument is not a witness of this graph. A
full solver may allow suitable supertype bounds or conservative primitive
alternatives; each complete witness still retains the original rebind link.
This conditional witness disproves the claim that all such decorated-core
client relations can be factored into independently chosen ports/calls.
Whole copying retains this join; PG-1's projection-body coverage does not
extend to `Relay`.

### 5.4 Hidden admission and under-generalization: precise limits

In the conditional `Make` graph of §5.1, the captured descriptor may itself
be a latent provider. A future challenge of the returned `pick` observes the
provider actually captured and
returned along that history. Consequently its source path/environment and
typed future-use admission depend on the captured tuple, under the graph's
typed premises. This makes no claim about the candidate brace spelling. The
syntactic transitive-dependency sufficient condition for admission-uniform
hiding in certified-use §4.1 cannot discard it. This identifies a conditional
constructor-derived admission dependency; it is not another stipulated two-row marginal
countermodel and does not by itself prove a failed full Function comparison.

For under-generalization, `id 0` and `id (lambda y.y)` give independently
source-checked projection views with distinct parameter descriptor terms.
A representation that fixes `a` to one particular term and has no instance
extension for the other loses PG-1's reconstructed view. However, a shared
bound containing both descriptors can still contain both executions; the
two traces alone do not prove loss of principal precision. Showing a
principality failure requires a distinguishing independently valid complete
client view and the failed designated-export extension above. No false
“different runtime values imply incompatible shared type variable” argument
is used here.

Finally, an undirected closure over every co-occurrence in a `Fun` term would
put `a` and captured `b` of `pick` in one block and could unnecessarily fix
`a`. The projection source family shows why that closure is too strong for
candidate selection: arbitrary `T` is admissible as the parameter interface
while the original capture stays fixed. This is not permission to ignore
actual residual equalities, provider dependence or §22 comparisons.
