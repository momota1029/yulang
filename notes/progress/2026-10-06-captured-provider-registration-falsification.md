## Objective and result

Attack the shortcut that turns the approved lexical capture in
`my apply f = { my step x = f x; step }` into its original typed
provider/contract/profile association by applying the existing closure,
name, transport or comparison results.

The smallest unsupported step is **admitting the captured lexical root as
an already decorated provider root for source-contract §3.2**. The Name
and Lambda clauses consume the §3.1 decorated source input. That input
already includes the typed paths, ownership/receipt and original shared
tuple that registration is supposed to construct. Neither retaining the
lexical root nor copying its ordinary endpoint establishes this premise.

Claim classes:

- Established requirements: the exact source resolves and retains outer `f`;
  registration must arise from its source component, preserve original
  position/contract/scope and joint `nu,K,D`, and remain independent of `Q`.
- Bounded characterization: the inspected emission/transport/comparison
  clauses consume the missing decorated association; no inspected clause
  supplies its formation from the approved lexical facts alone.
- Conditional theorem: in the restricted finite proof fragment below,
  every derivation using those clauses to transport an original profile
  witness depends on a supplied source-profile premise; ordinary lexical
  retention cannot supply that witness.
- Candidate assumption, still open: a Q-independent source producer can
  construct that association for this captured formal/use component.

This is an unclosed proof premise, not an accepted-source counterexample,
language impossibility theorem, complete registration rule, or principality
result. No production acceptance or source rejection is asserted.

## Minimal failed certificate

Use proof labels without selecting `beta`, compiler records or runtime
storage:

```text
b_f       resolved outer formal
l_step    local closure origin
u_f       captured name in that closure body
c         its f x call occurrence
Lex       resolve(u_f)=b_f and l_step retains that capture
Reg       source-certified original association of b_f/provider with its
          shared inferred contract, beta/profile, scope and joint nu,K,D
```

`Lex` is established by the addendum. `Reg` names the missing proof
conclusion; it does not assume a storage layout or a particular slot
inventory. The attempted certificate is:

```text
Lex
------------------------------------ claimed use of source-contract §3.2
Lambda/Name retains the original dependent provider root;
therefore that root has the completed original Reg
```

Inverting that rule application exposes its missing premise:

```text
DecoratedSource(source, original provider roots, typed paths/receipts, nu,K,D)
  + the applicable source-constructor/local-typing certificate
  + Lex
----------------------------------------------------------- §3.2 emission
retain the supplied original provider roots at Name/Lambda
```

Section 3.1 supplies the listed decorations; §3.2 states their emission
inventory; §2.2 requires the provider predicates to be semantically active
at those typed incidences. Inserting a lexical binder label for that
decorated input changes the premise. Even after supplying the §3.1 fields,
§3.2's root-retention conclusion alone does not form `beta`/`Slots(beta)`:
the attempted “therefore” additionally strengthens the conclusion. Its
slot/profile formation still needs call-view §2's source producer. Inserting
`Reg` inside an assumed complete `Gamma` or calling the closure
“source-certified” assumes the desired conclusion.

One captured formal and one attempted original-association conclusion
suffice. No second activation, request, operation family, annotation,
recursive group or alternative provider is needed. Removing the capture
removes this particular seam; removing the association conclusion removes
the unsupported inference. This is minimal by proof-premise count, not a
minimized accepted program or observable runtime witness.

## Conditional dependency lemma

Hypotheses for the lemma:

H1. A finite ground proof uses only the inspected ordinary lookup/result/
closure skeleton, source-contract emission/certified transport, typed-view
transport and §5.3 certificate clauses. Unknown formation rules are excluded.

H2. Independently typed primitive/kernel inputs, decorated `Gamma` entries,
profiles, registered recursive roots and original predicate operands are
explicit premises. An original profile input counts as a source-profile
premise even if represented by a reference rather than an inline tuple.
The source-contract §3.1 envelope alone is not assumed to contain that profile.

H3. The target contains a transported profile witness attached to the
original provider association under its original jointly scoped `nu,K,D`.
No independent per-port witness choice or Q-derived leaf is admitted.

Then a derivation in this fragment of that profile witness has a supplied
source-profile premise in its dependency ancestry. This is a provenance
claim for the original profile witness, not a claim that every coordinate
of `Reg` must be supplied in one complete leaf.

Proof by last-rule analysis and induction on finite proof height:

1. Ordinary Name copies an interface already in `Gamma`; §6 parameter
   formation constructs its Value/Computation role and endpoint skeleton,
   explicitly leaving detailed path/profile premises admitted. Its fresh
   endpoint is not a new original provider/profile certificate. Result and
   closure skeleton construction preserve their supplied environment.
2. Source-contract Name/Lambda/Result emission retains original dependent,
   captured or returned roots from the decorated input. Bind/Call retain
   their supplied typed operands and current-state/receipt obligations.
   None of these inventory clauses introduces `beta`/`Slots(beta)` from
   lexical facts. If a profile accompanies an emitted root, its original
   profile witness is in a supplied typed premise or a child, and induction
   applies. Root retention alone has no such profile conclusion.
3. Typed-boundary §6 uses relational image. Every transported profile has
   an input boundary/profile witness along a typed correspondence; `K` stays
   the shared ledger and `D` moves with its original predicate incidences.
   Dynamic introduction takes a supplied source profile. It does not derive
   that profile from lexical capture. Receipt adds ownership of use and
   explicitly creates no boundary or concrete contract.
4. Source-contract §5.3 Guarantee/Absorb requires exactly the same provider
   operand and non-coverage envelope; constructor congruence checks matching
   original provider links, paths and scopes. These are applicability checks,
   not provider-link generators. A registered recursive reference likewise
   consumes its original root/label certificate; a cycle is not a base case
   for a finite proof. No recursive-origin or guard conclusion follows.
5. A primitive or supplied decorated leaf carrying the original profile
   is exactly the source-profile premise counted by H2. A genuinely new
   primitive formation proof would escape H1 and would be the missing
   source producer, rather than a contradiction of this lemma.

Consequently a chain consisting solely of these preservation results cannot
derive the original profile witness from `Lex` alone. A successful chain
still requires the initial profile/association producer. This theorem concerns proof dependencies
of the stated fragment. It does not prove that every possible Yulang source
rule is incapable of producing registration, or that the future producer
must use a particular representation.

## Discriminator, independence and stopping point

The discriminator is rule-premise inspection: require a candidate formation
proof to show the first source clause introducing `Reg`, and classify each
leaf as approved lexical fact, derived source constraint, or supplied typed
certificate. If its first such clause is Name/Lambda emission or typed
transport, inversion reveals the assumed decorated certificate above.
If it presents an independent formation clause with a proved source premise,
this audit leaves that clause for review rather than rejecting it.

Named proof mutation: replace the decorated source-contract input by `Lex`
while leaving §3.2's conclusion unchanged. The rule becomes inapplicable
under §§3.1–3.2. A second illicit step, strengthening root retention to
completed slot/profile formation, is not justified by the inventory even
when its actual envelope is supplied. These mutations were reasoned about,
not executed. They are neither
an alternative accepted Yulang semantics nor a countermodel to approved a2.
No speculative two-provider model, receiver revival or runtime trace is
used. Later source-call activation is a separate consumer of registration;
typed-core §9 requires actual boundary/receipt/current-state premises and
cannot repair the initial formation cut.

There is no executable oracle. Approved decisions independently constrain
the source meaning and required formation direction. The conditional packages
share supplied typed-source/kernel/profile assumptions; chaining them gives
conditional consequences, not independent validation of those assumptions.
A checker accepting their transitions would not prove the omitted source
formation rule. Independent review is recorded below.

The earlier constructor and registration attempts already leave the same
formation premise open. This lane stops after identifying its exact first
rule-applicability failure. Repeating a name/application table or enlarging
a supplied-transition toy model would leave it untouched.

Failure conditions for this result: a previously overlooked inspected clause
actually derives the original association from the approved source inputs;
the candidate supplies a new independently grounded formation rule; or a
direct semantic dependency changes. In those cases recheck the affected
last-rule cases. Unrelated compiler/task movement does not supply that rule.
