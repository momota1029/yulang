## 3. What endpoint closure actually establishes

**Closure calculation.** If `s in R` and `E(s,t)`, then `t in R`.
Indeed s belongs to some finite Rk, so t belongs to R(k+1). This retains
the edge's whole tuple and sigma; it chooses no new witness. The same
argument works for any component sort and every legal original event.
The set E may have infinitely many event/development instances despite
its finite presentation. Each individual insertion has a finite rank.

Consequently a genuine positive child edge

```text
E_child((A,a,r,e; sigma), (B,a,r,e; sigma))
```

does provide `R.V(A,a,r,e) -> R.V(B,a,r,e)`. This action is obtained from
the closure equation itself, rather than from an ordinary V* theorem.
Its use alone asserts no readout for either tuple. The old arbitrary-S
counterexample therefore does not refute this construction, and this
calculation does not prove its newly inserted tuples sound.

**Conditional postfixed calculation.** Suppose base B has Phi(R) readouts
and Compat_R holds for every activated E edge. Induct on insertion rank.
The base case is the supplied base readout. For a new target t, its source
s has smaller rank; induction gives Phi(R)(s), and closure membership gives
s in R. Compat_R then gives Phi(R)(t). Hence `R subset Phi(R)`.
Only after this calculation may greatest-fixed-point introduction be used.
The assumption Compat_R is not discharged by this calculation.

For a base containing B_H, its non-hole readouts follow from the selected
background lift once `B_H subset R`; every declared source hole still needs
its actual Phi(R) source readout. Closing endpoints does not replace those
hole proofs. Generalizing the selected two-hole background to cO/cE is
also an application/embedding obligation.
