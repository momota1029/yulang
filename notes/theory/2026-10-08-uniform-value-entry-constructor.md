## 3. Why the fixed equality proof is insufficient

For a fixed-description constructor, let `CarrierContract(U)=I0` with
`I0.result=Unit`. An independent Bool carrier t1 is accepted at I1, where
`I1.result=Bool`, and is a compatible I1 challenge. A permitted fixed inlet
interpretation can satisfy

```text
Acc(I0,t0)=true;  Acc(I0,t1)=false;  Acc(I1,t1)=true.
```

Take pure returning t0/t1, no captures, requests, raw handles or latent values,
and one jointly compatible world. Such a fixed U may realize the Unit
description. Rewriting the Bool certificate by `CarrierContract(U)=I1` is
invalid: U is fixed and I0 and I1 differ on t1. The Bool realization fails its
actual-admission clause before receipt. This is a two-frame countermodel to
the *equality shortcut*, not intended Yulang rejection behavior or a compiler
execution result.

If I0 were genuinely stored in fixed OneShot/Established data and had a free
Parameter Desc dependency, Generalize's own fix walk would fix that dependency.
It could not both keep the semantic I0 fixed and make its payload declaration
an unconstrained template. Alternatively I can denote a schematic typing
interface whose interpretation varies by a description frame. That requires
an explicit source-owning constructor separating its schematic field from the
fixed actual inlet. The next sections give such a constructor without assigning
a schematic meaning to an arbitrary previously fixed U.
