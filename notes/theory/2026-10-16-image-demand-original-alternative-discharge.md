## 6. Solution-preservation traps and exact disposition

Three tempting replacements do not establish the required theorem:

1. Conjoin U to a retained original alternative's outputs. This filters the
   relation; it does not prove **every** originally admitted output's image
   satisfies U. In particular it can discard the very original witness the
   complete upper must cover. No actual concrete counterexample is claimed
   because the concrete Z primitive is unselected.
2. Keep the implication as an opaque atom in the scheme. This can preserve
   an unresolved obligation if all its dependencies remain active; it is the
   already required pending-retention protocol, not effective discharge.
3. Replace the opaque alternative with its declared marginal support, its
   source anchor or a conveniently chosen provider. This discards the
   original raw-current-state/future correlation or illegally requires a
   source execution for Z. The selected IF/Option 2 meaning excludes it.

The result is an **absent primitive closure contract**, not a proof of
undecidability or of intrinsic impossibility for the proposed architecture.
