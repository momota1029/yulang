complete-value result clause can differ from the diagonal clause only when an

with different constant results and one future invocation. It is conditional:
it requires complete shared interface membership, an actual result-replacement
license, admission correspondence, final guard survival, and separation after
the exhaustive Option 2 grammar and final projection. This note constructs
the conditional query consequence and identifies those missing suppliers. It
does not construct a source-admitted counterexample or an executable experiment.

## 3. Minimal source-shaped observation

The following is a source sketch for the observation, not a checked acceptance
claim or a new source syntax rule:

```text
my id x = x
my zero u = 0
my one u = 1
my result = (id zero) ()
```

`one` is the prospective alternative provider. It need not occur in the
actual client expression. Let `p0` and `p1` denote the whole provider packages
of the two closures, under their original typed transport. Their inequality
will be established behaviorally by returning different integers, not inferred
from distinct endpoint IDs.

For the actual source invocation, fix one original assignment `xi=(nu,K,D)`
and a pure argument carrier whose demand returns `p0`. The source rules give

```text
receipt; demand returns p0; rebind x := Value(p0);
lookup x; return p0; future invoke returned value on () => 0.
```

This is a derivation relative to the admitted typed primitives and lexical
interfaces of typed core §§6/9. It neither proves all source checking premises
nor selects a public descriptor. In particular the body cannot return `p1`.
The presence of two constant closures does not itself license replacing one
by the other in any production observation.

Hold fixed a proposed complete value interface `q*` with the ordinary Pure
callable role, Value Unit entry and Value Int result, and with every admitted
carrier, receiver, bound, authority, dependency and future-use condition
explicit. A pure body alone does not imply a pure complete invocation for
arbitrary argument carriers. The concrete future carrier here is pure Unit.
Assume both `Val_q*(p0)` and `Val_q*(p1)` under that very interface. This is an
explicit missing common-view constructor premise, not evidence obtained by
printing `Unit -> Int`. Actual provider executions remain the distinct
constant executions even if one common guarantee bounds both.

The caller, its carriers, actual receivers, original `xi`, projection and
instantiation `q := q*` are identical in both comparisons. Only the candidate
canonical result clause varies:

```text
Ret_diag: returned package is p0 through typed identity transport.
Ret_val:  p0 and returned package both satisfy q*, with independent license.
```

The intended complete-value alternative returns `p1`, then exposes `1` at
the result port of the one future invocation. It is an abstract alternative
only; it is not an alternate reduction of the source `id`.

## 4. Exact conditional theorem

Let `r_d` and `r_v` be actual transformed roots decoded from the same scheme
`forall q. q -> q`, with their respective result laws and the candidate's
complete `H_G` membership construction. The following are hypotheses, not
established semantic suppliers:

1. **Common interface and typing.** Both provider packages satisfy the same
   complete `q*`; both roots have active independently interpreted ordinary
   descriptors, and their constructor-typing obligations hold. Original
   role/entry/result consumer and all non-coverage operands are fixed.
2. **Licensed alternative.** At the fixed received `p0`, `Ret_val` has a
   concrete whole result/provider transport witness returning `p1`. Its
   authority, scope, ownership, dependencies and future-provider conditions
   hold independently of a query or matching type labels. Identity transport
   is also licensed. The two providers' actual future executions above hold.
3. **Independent admission.** The initial challenge and the future Unit call
   at the returned typed result port are admitted. Where a whole-query domain
   comparison is used, the complete domains of `r_d` and `r_v` have a certified
   equality. This includes every response, resumption and admission-live
   abstract provider, not just the displayed two-step history. Substituting
   the returned provider is accompanied by an original-scoped typed history
   correspondence; raw provider IDs need not define the common history.
4. **Complete separation.** A complete observation `y1` of the licensed
   alternative and its future result `1` survives `G_v`, including active
   `DescMem`, and enters full `M_v`. Its final projection survives as `o1`.
   For the corresponding admitted history `h`, no full `M_d` witness projects
   to `o1`. This quantifies over all diagonal-root source/base, unanchored `Z`
   and finite `W` alternatives and guards. Merely excluding `p1` from
   `Ret_diag` is insufficient: `W/Z` could replace the provider or abstract
   its future result while preserving the diagonal base clause.
5. **Actual-root query formation/conformance.** `r_d` is a valid completed
   required public view and §5.3's finite local resolution rule is sound and
   available at these submitted roots. No hidden source Lambda supplies the
   query. The self-comparison has the requisite finite primitive/constructor
   identities, complete equation pairing and original scope map.

**Conditional result.** The fixed public query target `r_d` admits the
diagonal candidate's self-query by domain `Eq` and membership reflexivity.
The complete-value candidate cannot have a sound membership `Le(M_v,M_d)`:
the witness from 2–4 projects to `o1` in `P_v(h;xi)` but not in `P_d(h;xi)`.
Consequently it cannot receive a certificate for that same whole query under
§5.3's rule. This is a possible loss of query precision for the conservative
candidate, not a demonstrated runtime safety failure of the actual source.

The proof uses one introduced alternative and one future invocation.
Reflexivity constructs the diagonal query certificate only under 1/5; it does
not establish descriptor formation. The off-diagonal observation refutes even
semantic observation containment under 2–4, so enlarging a finite proof search
cannot repair it. Domain equality in 3 is a separate supplier and is not proved
by positivity or by the observation witness.

This query is a mathematical public-view construction, conditional on 5.
No selected source annotation or source client has been shown to express its
correlation demand. Thus it is not a source-admitted acceptance discriminator.

## 5. Minimality, failure conditions and independence

For this behavioral method with provider identity erased by the initial value
projection, two behaviorally different providers and one future invocation
are necessary. With one provider or equal future integer results, this
observation cannot discriminate. If provider correlation itself were an
independently admitted public observation, the return alone could suffice;
no such observation is assumed here. This is minimality within the stated
one-future-call method, not a global minimal-program theorem.

The main failure conditions are explicit: common `q*` membership may fail;
the unequal transport may have no license; its future input may be outside
admission; the complete guard or ordinary descriptor may exclude `y1`; final
projection may erase the difference; the diagonal grammar may already contain
`o1`; or `r_d` may have no public formation/query supplier. Each failure
removes this discriminator without changing the selected source fact.

Named mutations are documentary, unexecuted: erase the independent license;
drop the future-domain certificate; replace the complete guard by type labels;
ignore `Z/W`; erase future integer results at projection; or infer identity
solely from repeated `q`. The argument supplies no source-validating oracle.
It shares admitted typed-core laws and the stated candidate suppliers with
its target. Running a checker that assumes these rules would establish only
consistency with those rules, not their source validity. No seeds, ranges,
search enumeration or mutation execution occurred.

The blocker is precise: no independently licensed unequal result package with
complete admission and post-abstraction projection separation has been supplied.
The selected contracts also do not prohibit such a package. This is stronger
than the candidate's bare unequal-provider clause test, and its missing premise
cannot be resolved by another equivalent finite toy probe.

Recommended next action: assign a source/primitive correspondence lane to
construct or exclude the whole unequal-result license together with its
admission certificate under a fixed exhaustive `W/Z/G`. Return the result to
the primary before treating the two clauses as a user-visible semantic choice.
