## Fixed source and symbolic typed-core nodes

Use labels `b_f`, `b_x`, `b_step` for the outer formal, local formal, and local sequential binding. Labels are source positions, not logical binder objects, allocation decisions, or static slot identities. The fixed lexical map is `use(f) -> b_f`, `use(x) -> b_x`, final `use(step) -> b_step`; the inner Call belongs to the local closure body. Both formals have ordinal zero in their respective headers, without identifying their declarations.

The exact hypotheses for the following symbolic construction are:

1. **H_env:** §6's ordinary parameter interfaces are represented by fresh symbolic value endpoints, `P_f=Value(A_f)`, `P_x=Value(A_x)`. The inner lexical environment retains the original outer endpoint relationship for `b_f`, rather than inventing an independently typed captured provider. This is a conditional environment representation, not a selected local-generalization policy.
2. **H_core:** the reviewed Draft §6 structural synthesis rules are used for this finite graph. Known source computation ports carry their original path obligations; the Call and Bind endpoint constraints remain obligations. No satisfiability or descriptor/admission proof is assumed by merely allocating their endpoint names.
3. **H_admin, only for contraction:** the existing same-context delay/force law applies to the typed instance, preserving paths and crossing no receipt, handler, or return delimiter. The literal skeleton can retain the administrative pairs and does not need their contraction to be written.

Let `C=call(n_f,n_x)` and `S=Fun(P_x,Comp(E_c,A_c))`. The table is bottom-up and records §6's entire `(I,d,n)` triple, rather than confusing inert `d` with its designated consumer `n`.

| Fixed source node | Interface `I` | Inert derivation `d` | Consumer `n=Normalize(I,d)` | What is actually obtained |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inner `f` name | `Value(A_f)` | `name b_f` | `n_f=result(name b_f)` | Conditional name skeleton sharing the captured lexical relationship; no independent Function/provider typing. |
| Inner `x` name | `Value(A_x)` | `name b_x` | `n_x=result(name b_x)` | Conditional value-result skeleton, `Comp(empty,A_x)`; no complete incoming-carrier bound. |
| Inner `f x` | `Computation(E_c,A_c)` | `reify(C)` | `n_c=eliminate_p_c(reify(C))` | Call/result/consumer nodes with symbolic complete-invocation endpoints and unsupplied callable/whole-argument/path obligations. |
| Local function `step x = f x` | `Value(S)` | `L_x=lambda(P_x,n_c)` | `r_x=result(L_x)` | Conditional closure/body-result skeleton, inert on introduction; complete callable membership and capture evidence remain unsupplied. |

The declaration names the outer closure; the exact treatment of a module export/generalization is not chosen by this expression ledger. The parameter table fixes the outer parameter roles of `apply` and `step`. It does not determine the actual role or entry of the callable later supplied as `f`.

Under H_env and H_core, writing the uncontracted spine requires no solved endpoint or fresh source semantic clause:

```text
lambda(P_f,
  eliminate_p_b(reify(
    bind(b_step,
      result(lambda(P_x,
        eliminate_p_c(reify(
          call(result(name b_f), result(name b_x)))))),
      result(name b_step)))))
```

Under H_admin it contracts in the original respective contexts to the addendum's intended spine:

```text
lambda(P_f,
  bind(b_step,
    result(lambda(P_x,
      call(result(name b_f), result(name b_x)))),
    result(name b_step)))
```

Neither line is a fully decorated typing derivation. `E_c,A_c,E_b` are constrained symbolic endpoints, not row unions, empty-effect claims, selected solutions, or a principal scheme. `Comp(empty,A_x)` describes the rebound name lookup; an incoming carrier to `step` may have effects or diverge. Likewise the closure body/result skeleton `Fun(P,Result(I_body))` is not a closed bound on the complete invocation with arbitrary carriers. No beta/Slots or callable-role refinement is inferred from these shapes.
